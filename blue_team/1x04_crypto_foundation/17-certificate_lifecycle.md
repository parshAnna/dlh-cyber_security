## 1. Executive decision and scope

**Decision:** Make certificate ownership, centralized inventory, automated issuance/renewal, independent expiry monitoring and clinical-safe validation mandatory. **Use a publicly trusted Let's Encrypt certificate with ACME automation for the real public patient-portal hostname**, not the `.local` training hostname. Use a governed **internal enterprise CA or approved private PKI for internal-only services and device identities**, commercial/public CA when external trust or a contractual requirement justifies it, and vendor-/Microsoft-managed certificates only within their respective service boundaries. No individual certificate can be considered safe simply because an administrator plans to renew it.

**Why urgent:** 1x02 **Finding 013** and 1x04 Task 0 reported a **Let's Encrypt portal certificate with 18 days left at the original Week 5 audit** and **no automatic renewal**; **this does NOT establish an absolute expiry date or its remaining lifetime on 6 November 2026**. `web-srv-01` is the patient portal, serving approximately **800 patient visits/connections a day** as the scenario states; a trust error may interrupt appointments or access to clinical information. **Finding 005** independently reports support for obsolete TLS 1.0. The renewal fix and the TLS-version fix require separate acceptance tests. Task 10 created a **lab-only `.local` CSR**, not a public certificate, and Task 11 tested third-party websites, not the live MedDefense portal. [M1–M5]

**Boundary of evidence:** Only the portal **issuer family (Let's Encrypt)** and **relative expiry at the historical audit** are reported. No inventory of real SANs, serials, exact `notAfter` timestamps, AD CS deployment, VPN authentication mode, EHR service certificate, O365 S/MIME certificates, or code-signing service is supplied. `Unknown` below means **discover/validate**, never an invented issuer or future expiry. Private CA root/intermediate certificates and Microsoft service-managed TLS certificates are different ownership models.

### 2.2 Certificate discovery and enrollment rules

1. Within **48 hours** of adopting the plan, verify the public portal from **outside the hospital**, with SNI, across all IPs/load-balancers; independently check the deployed live chain rather than trusting a local certificate file. Compare UTC expiry with the original finding; mark unknown until measured.
2. Within **10 business days**, enumerate internal HTTPS, PostgreSQL/MySQL TLS endpoints, AD/LDAPS, NAS management, DICOM interfaces and VPN certificate/PSK modes. Capture actual trust/renewal capability, without probing or changing active clinical devices without approval.
3. Every issued or imported certificate needs a **named owner and deputy** and a recorded expiration value **before deployment**. A newly found certificate with unknown owner is escalated to Sarah and James; a private key without an owner is not copied into an analyst workstation.
4. Distinguish **issued certificates**, **planned certificates**, **provider-managed certificates** and **not-applicable/no-certificate** cases. Do not mark a proposed AD CA, S/MIME rollout, code-signing service or VPN certificate as installed just to fill inventory.
5. Check **public Certificate Transparency records** and approved DNS names as an additional discovery signal, but CT does not enumerate all internal or private-CA certificates; confirm deployed leafs, intermediates, endpoints and SANs directly.

## 3. Part 2 — Auto-Renewal Strategy and CA Selection

| Option | Operations, cost and trust | 2026 lifetime reality | Fit for MedDefense |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACME / Let's Encrypt** | **Free DV public TLS**; ACME provides automated domain validation, issuance, renewal and revocation; use an ACME client with **ARI support** when available. Trust depends on valid public DNS ownership, certificate chain and compatible browsers. | **90-day default in Nov 2026**. Let's Encrypt announced **64-day default starting 10 February 2027**, followed by **45-day default on 16 February 2028**; do **not** hard-code a 90-day renewal interval. [P1–P2] | **Preferred for public patient portal**, other public web endpoints with supported ACME integration; test a renewal and deployment hook end-to-end. |
| **Commercial public CA** | Paid DV/OV/EV or specialized contractual service; support/SLA or enterprise features may justify it, but **manual renewal adds operational risk**. Automated ACME is preferable where offered. | The assignment's “manual, paid, **1-year**” is an outdated default for new public TLS: CA/Browser Forum maximum **200 days from 15 March 2026**, **100 days from 15 March 2027**, and **47 days from 15 March 2029**. [P3] | Exception only for externally required identity assurance, special enterprise contract or verified interoperability; **still automate renewal** and monitor. |
| **MedDefense internal CA / private PKI** | Organization-operated CA for internal server/client, LDAP and medical systems where approved, with HSM/KMS protected signing keys, offline root, audit, CRL/OCSP/trust propagation. | Validity governed by a documented private policy and application constraints; public-trust rules do **not automatically apply** to a private trust anchor. | Internal EHR, AD, VPN certificate identities and vendor-approved PACS interfaces, after PKI operations and recovery capability are funded. |
| **Microsoft/provider-managed** | Microsoft manages Exchange Online service leafs; MedDefense manages its **own tenant/custom certificates and S/MIME if deployed**. | Provider lifecycle rather than MedDefense leaf renewals; custom certs have their own policy. | **Do not attempt to replace provider-owned certificates**; monitor tenant integration and preserve contract/service contacts. |

**Patient-portal decision:** Use **Let's Encrypt + ACME with an approved, publicly registered FQDN**, automated scheduled renewal and a secure deploy hook. `portal.meddefense.local` in T10 is a training CSR: **`.local` cannot receive a publicly trusted Let's Encrypt certificate**. Secure DNS-01 if the portal requires isolated validation or load-balancer independence (restrict DNS API token by zone/record and scope); HTTP-01 is acceptable if external validation and proxy routing work reliably. Wildcard issuance is **not a default requirement**. Prefer an **ECDSA P-256** key (T10) after validating browser/client and intermediary support; use a separately tracked RSA certificate only for a demonstrated compatibility need, not to preserve TLS 1.0. Restrict the ACME account and leaf-key permissions to web PKI operators. [M2, M4, P4]

**Renewal workflow (proposed, never claimed deployed):** (1) scheduled ACME check **at least daily**; use CA Renewal Info (**ARI**) when supported and otherwise renew at approximately **two-thirds of certificate lifetime elapsed** (a 90-day leaf has ~30 days left; 64-day has ~21 days left); (2) validate ownership/CAA/DNS and safely issue to a **staging or canary** endpoint; (3) install entire chain with correct SAN/EKU, restricted key and server configuration; (4) **atomic rollout and reload** of every proxy/node; (5) re-query **live SNI endpoints** from outside and inside, verify chain/hostname/notAfter, supported TLS 1.2/1.3 and patient login/API workflows; (6) log success, new serial and deployed nodes, update inventory, close alert; (7) on failure retry with backoff, alert and **rollback only to a still-valid trusted certificate**, never TLS 1.0. Test ACME's staging environment and **actual deploy hook/reload** ahead of production. The same procedure requires a defined backup operator, credentials and recovery path during staff leave or CA/DNS outage. [P1, P4–P5]

**Clinical impact:** With approximately **800 daily patient sessions** dependent on the portal, expired leafs may trigger browser distrust, loss of patient access and delayed workflows. Owners should schedule low-impact changes, test authorized legacy clinical interfaces, provide an approved fallback access process, and report patient-care disruption to clinical leadership and James. **No blanket certificate-warning bypass or temporary cleartext service** is an acceptable recovery method.

## 4. Part 3 — Monitoring, Thresholds, Recipients and Escalation

**Monitoring owner:** Sarah's IT operations team operates a **central certificate monitor**, for example **Zabbix with certificate/HTTP probes** if approved; alternatively an existing supported enterprise monitor with equivalent features. **Do not assume Zabbix is already installed.** An **independent external TLS probe** must measure the *actual* portal certificates from every public endpoint/SNI route; the central monitor also inventories internal PEM/PFX/keystore certs and CA/intermediate/root expiry. Validate alert delivery independently of portal availability. Forward events into the proposed **C3/Wazuh SIEM if deployed**; **do not use the unregistered vulnerable Grafana 8.2.0 device (F029) as the trusted monitoring service** without remediation and ownership. [M3, P6]

**Collection:** External public TLS **every 6 hours** (hourly for portal if feasible); internal critical certificate state **daily**; check renewal job outcome after each run; **daily inventory-to-endpoint reconciliation** for critical certs and a monthly enterprise full reconciliation. Measure `days_until_expiry` from **UTC `notAfter`**, along with chain validity, SAN/hostname, EKU, issuer and fingerprint changes, renewal-job failures, deployment drift and missing telemetry. Alert **immediately** if leaf is already expired, revoked, wrong-host/untrusted, or a scheduled renewal has repeatedly failed—**do not wait for a day threshold**.

**Important short-lifetime exception:** T−90 cannot occur for a newly issued 90-day or 64-day certificate. T−60 may occur immediately after a 64-day certificate is issued. Keep the requested thresholds as **policy categories**, but combine them with **renewal-state conditions and percentage-of-lifetime schedules**, not naïve daily page triggers. For the forthcoming **45-day** default, T−30/T−7 and ARI remain actionable; a T−60 warning never applies. Dedupe/reopen alerts per **certificate serial and deployed endpoint**; suppress resolved warnings only after independent live verification. [P1–P2]

**Escalation examples:** A renew command succeeded but the load balancer still serves the old certificate: **failure**, keep T−30/T−7 alert open. A commercial certificate is at T−90: early planning, not a critical incident. A portal certificate is within seven days **now** based on live measurement: activate critical escalation regardless of the historical “18 days” note. **Let's Encrypt discontinued expiration notification emails in June 2025**; MedDefense must own independent monitoring. [P7]

## 5. Part 4 — Five Mandatory Certificate Policy Rules

1. **Trusted issuance and approved names:** Every production TLS service must use a certificate chaining to the **MedDefense-approved internal CA** (for approved private services) or an approved **publicly trusted CA** (for public endpoints), with accurate **SAN**, correct EKU and authorized DNS ownership. **Direct self-signed leaf certificates are prohibited in production**; an explicitly approved and distributed self-signed **private CA root** is not the same as an untrusted self-signed server leaf. `.local` laboratory certificates may **not** replace public-patient-portal trust.
2. **Mandatory inventory and ownership:** No certificate may be issued, imported, deployed, copied to another node or left active without a register ID, business/system owner and deputy, exact UTC expiration date, issuer, SAN, fingerprint, key type, all deployment endpoints, auto-renewal method and alert recipient. Reconcile installed artifacts **monthly** and critical public endpoints **daily**; retired certs and broken trust chains must be removed through controlled change.
3. **Automated renewal with tested validation:** All technically compatible public TLS leaves must use **ACME or a comparably tested automated process** with daily checks, monitored job status, safe renew/reload and external live-certificate verification. Internal CA, email/S/MIME, VPN and vendor devices require equivalent owned renewal runbooks and overlap windows even if automation is not technically supported. Trigger T−90/T−60/T−30/T−7 lifecycle actions as applicable to **actual certificate lifetime**; treat expiration/invalid trust as an incident.
4. **Approved cryptography and protected private keys:** Use approved TLS **1.3** (or strong **TLS 1.2** where justified), modern AEAD suites, **ECDSA P-256** or approved RSA strength according to validated compatibility; disable **TLS 1.0/1.1** and obsolete algorithms after safe migration testing. Keep private keys in protected keystores/KMS/HSM or equivalent restricted service storage, forbid plaintext in Git, scripts or emails, apply least privilege/dual control for CA signing keys and reissue keys after suspected compromise. **Cert validity does not prove TLS configuration is secure.**
5. **Revocation, recovery, audit and clinical-safe change:** For compromise, revoke and replace affected certificates, rotate keys, audit privileged access, and independently verify new trust/chain at all endpoints. Test renewal, CA/DNS outages, rollback, expired/incorrect-host negative scenarios and restore procedures **quarterly**; preserve clinical continuity with an approved fallback, never a browser-warning bypass or unencrypted emergency interface. Document change tickets, monitoring evidence, clinical/DPO consultation and independent acceptance sign-off before marking an issue closed.

**Governance:** James is the security-policy owner and incident accountable role; Sarah owns technical implementation, monitoring and change execution; named Web/PKI/AD/Network/M365 operators maintain certificates; the Security Analyst independently validates tests and alert evidence; clinical owners and Biomedical/vendors approve clinical dependencies; DPO/Legal reviews patient-information impacts; CEO approves material spend and formal high/critical risk acceptance. This applies the **1x03 T4 proposed RACI**, and does not pretend the appointments, PKI or platform have already been approved or deployed. [M6]
