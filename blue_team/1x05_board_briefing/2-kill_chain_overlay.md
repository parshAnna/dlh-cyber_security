## Executive conclusion

**KC1 correctly predicted the main arc:** a vulnerable VPN creates an internal foothold, weak segmentation enables movement toward Active Directory, privileged access enables ransomware, and hospital-wide service disruption follows. **Crimson Tide adds specific branches** that KC1 did not explicitly model: FortiGate **CVE-2023-27997** as a named pre-authentication entry, **Kerberoasting of RC4/DES-enabled service accounts**, **theft of unencrypted EHR records before encryption/extortion**, and **direct destruction of reachable NAS backups**. KC1's objective was domain-wide ransomware; Crimson Tide explicitly pursues both **data theft** and **recovery denial**.

**Today:** 1x05 Task 0 provisionally scores **5/7 EXPOSED, 2/7 PARTIALLY PROTECTED, 0/7 PROTECTED**. In 1x03, **C1 + C2 + C3 + C4 + C6** were *selected for proposed funding*, **not approved, purchased or proven deployed**. Therefore no additional present-day protection is credited. The original course-specific `cisa_advisory_crimson_tide.txt` could not be independently retrieved in Task 0: the seven phase names below are **consistent analyst working labels from Task 0**, not claimed verbatim CISA headings. Reconcile them with the original advisory before final Board sign-off.

**Counterfactual, deliberately narrow:** if the five selected controls were fully deployed, correctly scoped, and validated, **2/7 phase objectives have a plausible decisive break point** (unrestricted flat-network movement in phase 3, and *destruction of every recoverable backup copy* in phase 6); **5/7 phase objectives retain a feasible route** if their prerequisites are satisfied. This is **not** a claim that two entire attack stages are mathematically impossible or that five attacks will actually succeed: local backup deletion and authorized-path lateral movement remain possible, and successful interruption at phase 3 would also cut downstream stages of this *particular* linear path. A strict zero-bypass guarantee is **0/7**. Details and scoring definitions appear in Part 3.

## 1. Method and traceability

- **Actual 1x01 KC1 model:** [1x01 T10 — Kill Chains](../1x01_know_your_enemy/10-kill_chains.md), headed *“Kill Chain #1: VPN Foothold to Domain-Wide Ransomware”*; its five modeled steps are **Initial Access → Establish Foothold → Lateral Movement / Escalation → Objective Execution → Impact**.
- **Crimson Tide comparator:** [1x05 T0 — Advisory Analysis](0-advisory_analysis.md) defines seven *provisional* stages: (1) FortiGate SSL-VPN exploitation; (2) foothold, credential access and persistence; (3) flat-network discovery and lateral movement; (4) Kerberoasting and domain privilege; (5) patient-record exfiltration; (6) backup destruction; (7) ransomware and clinical disruption.
- **Evidence codes:** `F###` = 1x02 triage finding, `GAP-###` = 1x00 gap, `C-###` = existing control from 1x00, and **`C1`–`C8` = proposed** 1x03 control-cost options. Never confuse C-002 (existing VPN policy) with C2 (proposed MFA).
- **Match assessment:** **Exact concept** = predicted mechanism or direct progression; **Partial** = predicted broad objective but not the new specific technique/target; **New branch** = not separately modeled in KC1. A matching model is not evidence the attack actually took place.
- **Causal assumptions:** An Internet-reachable *SSL-VPN* listener is unverified. FortiOS **7.0.9** is in the affected release family, but a vulnerable version alone is not proof the entry condition exists or that an attacker succeeded. The independently reported IPsec site-to-site encryption does not patch SSL-VPN. A compromised authorized application can query plaintext even when its storage is encrypted.

### 1.1 Predictive accuracy and boundaries

**Three exact conceptual matches** are the VPN entry (phase 1), establishment of foothold (phase 2) and unrestricted internal movement (phase 3), plus an **exact final ransomware outcome** (phase 7). **Two partially predicted mechanisms** are Kerberoasting (phase 4) and targeted backup destruction (phase 6). **One explicit objective missing from KC1** is the intermediate patient-data exfiltration phase (phase 5). These counts concern **seven mapped phases**, not seven identical KC1 steps; phases 4–6 refine/extend KC1's five-step model.

**Cross-chain learning:** KC1 is the right overall ransomware model, **KC5** independently identifies EHR exfiltration as an objective, and **KC3** describes radiology/PACS extortion. Their existence does not mean the original KC1 specifically foresaw database theft or direct NAS deletion, or that the scenario's Crimson Tide actor actually executed KC2/KC3. **KC2** in 1x01 is a *negligent pharmacy change*, not a ransomware step, so “the FortiGate is on KC2” must be treated as a *possible downstream access dependency*, not a verbatim KC2 hop.

## Part 3 — The gap between plan and reality

**Counterfactual assessment (one paragraph):** If MedDefense had completely implemented, staffed and clinically validated the five **selected** 1x03 controls before the alert, the model would have had **two plausible decisive interception points out of seven**: **phase 3's unrestricted flat-network traversal** would be cut by C1 default-deny interzone rules, and **phase 6's objective of eliminating every recoverable backup** would be defeated by C4 independently immutable, tested offsite copies. Accordingly, **2/7 phase objectives are counted as blocked or neutralized in this narrowly defined best-case comparison**, while **5/7 remain capable of succeeding when their separate prerequisites exist**: initial SSL-VPN exploitation, host persistence, service-account password attack, authorized-path EHR exfiltration and at least localized ransomware/clinical disruption. **This does not mean the attacker will complete five later phases after a truly blocked phase 3**; that linear chain would break there unless it finds another entry/legitimate path. Nor does it mean all local backups are protected from deletion; C4 protects an *independent recoverable copy*, not NAS-01 itself. Because C1 also permits necessary authenticated EHR/AD traffic and C4 is a recovery control, the strict **guaranteed end-to-end prevention count remains 0/7**, rather than an invented certainty. The lesson is that the strategy substantially improves containment and survivability but leaves residual **pre-auth gateway**, **Kerberoasting/gMSA**, **live PHI access**, **server EDR and staffed response** risks. The selected controls are **still only proposed**, so the present-day Task 0 score remains **5 EXPOSED / 2 PARTIALLY PROTECTED** without verified new evidence.

### 3.1 Fast sensitivity check — what changes the count?

| Additional evidence/assumption | What changes | Effect on estimate |
|---|---|---|
| SSL-VPN disabled, not Internet-reachable, and verified | Phase 1 initial-access prerequisite absent | Gateway exploit *via that listener* becomes blocked today; don't assume this without evidence. |
| FortiGate correctly patched and SSL-VPN listener checked | Phase 1 CVE access neutralized | Adds **one direct preventive break point** when verified; not included in the five-control portfolio without separate approval. |
| Segmentation permits compromised trusted EHR/application service | Attacker bypasses direct phase 3 flat-network route | The C1 **“Yes”** becomes only **Partially** for adapted lateral movement; count falls to **1/7** strong objectives. |
| Backup immutability is only a shared NAS setting, or keys/admin credentials are co-compromised | No independent safe recovery copy | C4 **“Yes”** invalid; count falls to **1/7** (or **0/7** with a C1 bypass). |
| C3 sees no relevant ticket/EHR/export telemetry | Kerberoasting and exfil may be undetected | The already **partial** alerting effect vanishes; does not add another blocked phase. |

### 3.2 Evidence needed before anyone says “we blocked Crimson Tide”

1. **Entry:** Sarah produces read-only FortiGate SSL-VPN status and outside exposure test; vendor validates firmware and supported upgrade path. **Keep essential site-to-site IPsec functioning** and obtain clinical continuity clearance for restrictions.
2. **Foothold:** Security Analyst validates endpoint/server EDR coverage, named remote identities, MFA-denied tests, persistence detections and on-call response; do not assume the deferred C5 is live.
3. **Traversal:** Test denied VPN→AD/DB/NAS administration and explicitly allowed EHR, imaging, pharmacy and clinical flows; log zone-based rejects and clinical exceptions. Avoid unsafe medical-device scans.
4. **Identity:** Review Kerberos event 4769, SPN accounts, service-account password policy, DES/RC4 tickets and gMSA compatibility; ticket enumeration does not prove a password was cracked.
5. **Data theft:** Test authorized SQL/export pathways, role-specific grants, `pg_hba.conf` source/SSL restrictions and egress logging; record that at-rest encryption does not protect an authorized plaintext query.
6. **Recovery:** Demonstrate isolated recovery from a genuinely independent, immutable, encrypted offsite backup with separate keys and credentials; include **EHR, AD, PACS metadata/images, billing** and clinically acceptable restore evidence.
7. **Impact:** Run a controlled tabletop/purple-team exercise using synthetic data and approved test systems, measure alert-to-isolation time and clinical downtime workarounds, and update RISK-001/002/003/008 only with validated post-change evidence.
