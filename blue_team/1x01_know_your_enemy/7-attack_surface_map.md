This map cross-references the First Watch [Environment Summary](../1x00_first_watch/0-environment_summary.md), [Asset Registry](../1x00_first_watch/7-asset_registry.md), [Criticality Assessment](../1x00_first_watch/8-criticality_assessment.md), [Data Map](../1x00_first_watch/9-data_map.md), [Complete Control Matrix](../1x00_first_watch/10-complete_control_matrix.md) and [Gap Analysis](../1x00_first_watch/12-gap_analysis.md). The Network Scan Summary is source **S** in the Asset Registry. Current-project context comes from the [Social Engineering Analysis](4-social_engineering_analysis.md) and [Supply Chain Assessment](5-supply_chain_assessment.md).

An entry point is part of the attack surface even when a control protects it. A documented gap is not automatically proof of exploitation. The scan was conducted from an HQ workstation across connected networks; an open port in that scan proves reachability from that scan position, not exposure to the public Internet. Likewise, missing version or configuration evidence is reported as unverified rather than silently treated as a confirmed vulnerability.

## 2. Internal Surface

The dominant architectural condition is the reported flat Central network. The onboarding evidence describes a `10.10.0.0/16` broadcast domain with no VLANs, and source S states that the Central `10.10.1.0/24`, `10.10.2.0/24` and `10.10.3.0/24` groups share a physical network without firewall separation. S also reports broad reachability from Sarah Park's HQ workstation across the connected sites. This supports a serious segmentation gap, although it does not prove that every source can use every destination service or defeat application authentication.

## Surface Assessment Summary

The **internal surface represents MedDefense's greatest current risk** because it converts a limited initial compromise into a potentially organization-wide clinical event. External services and people provide credible entry routes, but the reported flat Central network, all-service VPN permissions, broad PostgreSQL/MySQL reachability, reachable management interfaces, unmanaged devices and legacy systems place the Top 5 assets—especially `ehr-db-01`, the core switch, infusion pumps and PACS—within an insufficiently separated environment. Existing perimeter filtering, workstation protection and local logs reduce some risk, yet they do not contain an attacker who arrives through the portal, VPN, email, a contractor or a compromised workstation. MedDefense should therefore treat segmentation and approved-flow enforcement under GAP-016 as the cross-surface priority, while preserving the service-specific actions for portal authorization, identity, device protection and recoverable backups.
