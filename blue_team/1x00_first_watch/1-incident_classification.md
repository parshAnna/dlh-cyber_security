# MedDefense Health Systems — Incident Classification

Prepared for: James Chen, Deputy CISO  
Prepared by: Junior Security Analyst  
Source: Task 1, “The First Incidents” — supplied incident log, January–June (year unspecified).

The primary CIA pillar represents the most direct or significant impact described in the log. Secondary impacts are included where supported; potential consequences are distinguished from confirmed outcomes. Integrity can be impaired by accidental errors as well as deliberate attacks. For Incident F, the primary classification identifies the pillar at risk because the log does not establish an actual compromise of organizational information.

| Incident | Primary CIA pillar | One-sentence justification | Secondary CIA pillar and connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — January 15: Billing ransomware** | **Availability — confirmed** | Ransomware encryption made billing-srv-01 unusable for insurance-claim processing for four days, directly interrupting Finance's access to a required service. | **Integrity — confirmed:** The payload altered billing data into encrypted form without authorization; the three-week-old backup also creates a potential recovery gap, although actual loss of recent records is not established. **Confidentiality is not established:** The log does not report data theft or unauthorized viewing. |
| **B — February 2: Patient portal access-control failure** | **Confidentiality — confirmed** | Broken access control allowed authenticated patients to view other patients' lab results by changing a URL parameter, exposing health information outside their authorized access. | **No secondary impact established:** The log describes unauthorized viewing, not modification of results or loss of portal availability. |
| **C — March 18: Incorrect medication dosages** | **Integrity — confirmed** | A defective database update script overwrote dosage values, causing the pharmacy management system to present inaccurate medication information across all three sites for approximately six hours. | **No secondary impact established:** The system remained accessible and no information disclosure is reported; unsafe dosage information creates a patient-safety risk, but actual medication errors or patient harm are not documented. |
| **D — April 5: Website defacement** | **Integrity — confirmed** | Replacing the public homepage with an unauthorized political message compromised the authenticity and correctness of MedDefense's published content. | **Availability — limited to legitimate homepage content:** The replacement prevented visitors from accessing the intended homepage until restoration within two hours, although a full website outage is not reported. **Confidentiality is not established:** The affected website is stated to contain no patient data, and the log reports no disclosure of other information. |
| **E — May 22: EHR migration outage** | **Availability — confirmed** | The EHR was inaccessible for nine hours during a prolonged database migration, forcing physicians to use paper records when electronic records were needed. | **No secondary impact established:** Paper-to-EHR reconciliation could introduce omissions or transcription errors affecting **Integrity**, but the log does not report any such errors or migration-related data corruption. |
| **F — June 10: Personal laptop on the internal network** | **Confidentiality — at risk; disclosure unconfirmed** | A personal laptop running file-sharing software spent three weeks on the internal network in the HR file share's segment, creating a potential route for unauthorized exposure of HR information without proving that HR files were accessed or shared. | **No secondary impact established:** Unauthorized modification could affect **Integrity**, and resource exhaustion or disruption could affect **Availability**, but neither is reported; sharing unspecified files and occupying the same network segment do not establish HR-share permissions, malware infection or organizational data leakage. |

**Assessment boundary:** These classifications describe the supplied events, not a forensic determination. Incident F requires evidence identifying the files shared, effective access permissions and any HR-file access before confidentiality loss can be confirmed. Incident D's availability effect concerns the replaced homepage content, not a proven outage of the entire site. No incident severity scores or unsupported breach conclusions are assigned.
