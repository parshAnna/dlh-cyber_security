# MedDefense Health Systems — Data Map

Prepared for: James Chen, Deputy CISO  
Prepared by: Junior Security Analyst  
Scope: Classification and lifecycle mapping from supplied project evidence; no live data inspection.

Task 9's Legal context establishes that MedDefense handles protected health information for **over 50,000 patients**; it does not establish an exact count of active EHR records, imaging studies or breach victims. Source references: **O** = onboarding documents; **I** = incident log; **D** = billing diagnostics; **W** = walk-through; **C A1–A8** = control artifacts; **M** = MRI scenario; **S** = scan summary, all indexed in the [Asset Registry](7-asset_registry.md). C-identifiers refer to the [Complete Control Matrix](10-complete_control_matrix.md).

Classification applies the supplied scale: Restricted for severe disclosure consequences, Confidential for significant harm, Internal for limited-impact non-public information, and Public for intended publication. Mixed datasets inherit their highest known sensitivity until fields are separated; unknown SSN/payment-card storage is not asserted as fact. The categories below are evidence-based analyst assignments, not a claim that MedDefense has already approved a formal classification policy. Known routes are distinguished from inferred functional flows; a listening port, an HTTPS option or a VPN label alone does not demonstrate end-to-end protection.

Safety-critical dosage/device values are established by Incident C and the medical-device inventory, but non-patient reference values are not automatically confidential merely because their integrity is Critical; patient-linked treatment information belongs with Restricted clinical records, and standalone reference/configuration data needs field-level classification and strong integrity controls.

## Data Risk Summary

MedDefense's most significant directly evidenced data-protection weakness is **Restricted patient medical information in use at shared, unattended clinical workstations**: W3 shows a patient record visible to passersby after at least 15 minutes, accompanied by an instruction discouraging logout, so disclosure can occur without malware, stolen network credentials or defeating storage encryption. Task 9 establishes PHI for over 50,000 patients across the organization, but does not prove that all records are accessible from that session; the exposed record and any additional data allowed by its account are the defensible scope. Generic awareness, perimeter filtering and local backups do not close this immediate gap between the Restricted classification and how staff display and handle the information. MedDefense should validate and enforce clinically workable session locking, individual access and protected screen placement/privacy filters, while separately addressing broad database access, incomplete transmission evidence and vulnerable recovery copies across the same data lifecycle.
