# MedDefense Health Systems — Complete Control Matrix

Prepared for: James Chen, Deputy CISO  
Prepared by: Junior Security Analyst  
Scope: Consolidation of available project evidence; no live testing or operational change.

## Board briefing

MedDefense has perimeter filtering, limited server hardening, workstation antivirus, local backups and partial physical protection. These controls do not establish adequate protection for the five highest-ranked clinical dependencies: EHR database, pharmacy system, Central core switch, infusion-pump fleet and PACS. Significant gaps include internal access restrictions, clinical-device protections, timely security monitoring and validated recovery. PACS is explicitly excluded from backup. Apparent control volume must not be confused with independent defensive layers or effective coverage.

The summary matrix and coverage map below provide the compact executive view; the detailed registry is the traceable supporting reference. No proposed compensating measure is presented as protection already deployed.

## Evidence and scoring conventions

Sources: **T4** = [original control inventory](4-control_inventory.md) and its eight artifacts; **O, I, D, W, M, S** = source keys in the [Asset Registry](7-asset_registry.md). The ranked assets follow [Task 8](8-criticality_assessment.md) exactly, including its explicitly grouped infusion-pump fleet.

C-001–C-038 preserve Task 4 IDs and classifications. New IDs extend the inventory with observed mechanisms, narrow historical recovery actions and support arrangements. Each record has one principal function; related functions of a product remain separate records, not separate products. A historical response demonstrates a limited capability, not a formal repeatable procedure. Evidence/Source states the reason for each effectiveness rating.

**Strong** requires evidence of appropriate configuration/scope and active maintenance; **Adequate** means present with limitations; **Weak** means poorly implemented, easily bypassed, or insufficiently operationalized. No control is rated Strong because the available snapshots/contracts do not demonstrate all three Strong criteria. This is an evidence-limited judgment, not a claim that every configuration is defective. An Adequate rating applies only to the stated scope: key-only SSH on ehr-srv-01 does not protect every Linux host. Unknown coverage is not silently credited.

For the summary, Weak = 1, Adequate = 2, Strong = 3. Average = sum of scores / control count in that cell; display bands are Weak below 1.50, Adequate from 1.50 to below 2.50, and Strong from 2.50 upward. These are transparent reporting conventions for ordinal labels, not quantitative risk measurements. Empty cells show 0 / N/A. Averages are unweighted by asset importance and must be read with the coverage map.

## Part 3 — Control Coverage Map

**Well-Protected:** evidenced controls adequately cover the asset's important prevention, detection and recovery needs. **Partially Protected:** meaningful protection exists but material gaps remain. **Under-Protected:** only weak, indirect or materially incomplete protection addresses the asset's critical needs. **Unprotected:** no relevant control is evidenced. These are assessments of documented coverage, not certifications of security or absence of all controls.

“Indirect” means perimeter, facility or workflow protection rather than a host-level control. “Not evidenced” is a documentation gap; “excluded/absent” is used only when a source explicitly says so. The requested map omits a Deterrent column, so any relevant deterrence is described in Coverage Assessment.

## Scope reconciliation and proposed controls

- **Task 6:** The [Task 6 MRI strategy](6-compensating_controls.md) now proposes CC-01–CC-04; no implementation evidence has been supplied. It does not evidence implemented MRI isolation, a jump host, application allowlisting or dedicated monitoring. Therefore the Compensating column remains empty. Track these designs as **Proposed**, outside deployed counts and coverage, until implementation evidence exists; do not assign Strong/Adequate/Weak operational effectiveness to an unimplemented proposal.
- **Data mapping:** No separate data-mapping deliverable was provided. Data locations and dependencies documented in Task 7 are included; encryption, DLP, retention enforcement and database permissions are not inferred merely from sensitive data or open HTTPS ports.
- **Physical walkthrough:** Unlocked closets and exposed records are weaknesses, not new protective controls. The generic badge reader and bypassed administrative door are counted because real access-boundary mechanisms exist, albeit Weak. Cameras/guard functions already in T4 are not counted again. UPS protection is not assigned to specific equipment without load mapping.
- **Identity and transport:** Windows GPO is reported rather than verified per host. Other Linux password authentication and shared PACS credentials are included as weak existing access gates. Westside IPSec is distinct from its permissive firewall policy: tunnel confidentiality does not provide service-level authorization. HQ VPN existence does not establish its cryptographic configuration. James's isolated MFA does not create organization-wide coverage.
- **Availability and response:** Historical website restoration, paper continuity and billing restarts are narrow demonstrated actions; no formal IR, BCP or DR plan exists in O. DC redundancy and RAID are separate limited availability measures, not substitutes for recovery copies. C-042 proves website restore capability only; C-024 proves a partial file-server test only.
- **Exclusions:** SIEM/Wazuh, offsite backups, medical isolation, role-specific/PHI training and server antivirus were absent, researched, requested or denied; none is counted as deployed. Vendor response SLAs do not establish resolution times or actual patch currency. Direct core-switch security and pharmacy-host coverage must remain unknown until evidence is obtained.

The immediate evidence priorities are to identify pharmacy hosting and core placement, establish PACS recovery, verify EHR restore capability, and document device-specific pump protections. Changes should then be tracked against these stable control IDs, with effectiveness updated only when scope and operation are demonstrated.
