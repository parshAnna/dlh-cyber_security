# Task 15 — Red Team Your Blueprint

**Organization:** MedDefense Health Systems  
**Project:** 1x03 — Defense Blueprint  
**Prepared for:** James Chen, Deputy CISO; Sarah Park, IT Director; Robert Kim, CFO; clinical risk owners  
**Assessment date:** 29 October 2026  
**Perspective:** BlackReef ransomware affiliate (defensive tabletop simulation)  
**Status:** Hypothetical residual-risk assessment; no attack, intrusion, deployment or control validation was performed.

## Scope and Assumptions

For **this exercise only**, assume the five controls funded in [Task 8](8-budget_allocation.md) have been **fully deployed, configured and tested**: **C2** VPN/admin MFA ($11,000/year), **C1** network segmentation ($30,000/year), **C3** Wazuh SIEM with assigned business-hours triage ($22,000/year), **C4** immutable offsite backups with proven recovery for their approved scope ($21,000/year), and **C6** a managed Westside firewall ($13,000/year). Total: **$97,000/year annualized**; illustrative first-year cash **$115,000**. This assumption intentionally overrides Task 8's actual *proposed/not-yet-authorized* status; it is **not a claim that MedDefense has implemented these controls**.

Do **not** assume the deferred **C5** estate-wide Sophos EDR upgrade ($41,000/year; $43,000/year *modeled marginal* reduction) or **C8** full medical-device isolation/monitoring ($44,000/year; $35,000/year *modeled marginal* reduction) is present. The **C7** $185,000/year outsourced 24/7 SOC was rejected, so Wazuh **does not** imply round-the-clock staffed response. Existing Sophos on some workstations, minimal medical-device safety safeguards and clinical change procedures may still exist; their actual effectiveness is not assumed away. The [Task 13 quick wins](13-quick_wins.md) are **proposals**, not automatically completed merely because T8 controls are assumed live.

**Adversarial method:** Evaluate trust granted to a *legitimate* user or approved clinical application, not an impossible direct crossing of the new firewall zones. This is a tabletop threat narrative, not operational attack instructions. A breached account, successful phishing message or software weakness is a **conditional premise**, not an observed fact.

## Part 1 — The Attacker's Perspective

### 1. Which of the Five Kill Chains Remains Viable?

**BlackReef's best remaining ransomware-oriented candidate is KC3: Radiology phishing to PACS extortion** ([1x01 T10](../1x01_know_your_enemy/10-kill_chains.md); **RISK-003**, with **RISK-006** as an adjacent legacy-controller concern). In the original chain, a Radiology user can be phished, shared PACS sessions weaken individual accountability (existing control **C-052**, **GAP-011**), and a compromised user may interact with imaging services over normal clinical channels. Fully functioning **C1** now blocks *unapproved* SMB/RDP and broad lateral movement, but it must still permit **authorized** Radiology/PACS viewing and modality traffic; application permissions and stolen approved sessions are not automatically made safe by a VLAN. **C2** covers VPN/admin MFA, not by definition every PACS user session; **C3** can alert on logs it actually receives and can be reviewed, but lacks C7's continuously staffed response; **C4** protects approved recovery copies and reduces extortion leverage without preventing access abuse, data exposure or temporary clinical downtime. As **C5** remains deferred, full supported endpoint/server behavioral coverage is not assured—although existing Sophos protection could still stop a particular attempt. Thus KC3 is **conditionally viable, harder and lower-impact than before**, not a guaranteed route to PACS control or successful encryption.

**Cross-check against all five source kill chains:**

| 1x01 chain | With all T8 selected controls functioning | Remaining limitation / verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **KC1 — VPN → AD ransomware** | C2 and C1/C6 constrain initial identity misuse, unauthorized AD paths and propagation; C3/C4 add detection and recovery | **Severely disrupted**, not impossible with stolen legitimate privileges or allowed application paths |
| **KC2 — Negligent pharmacy update → wrong dose** | Firewalls, VPN MFA, Wazuh and backups cannot validate the medical correctness of an *authorized* dosage-table change | **Still viable as a non-malicious control-failure chain**; **RISK-005**, GAP-002/GAP-022 |
| **KC3 — Radiology phishing → PACS extortion** | Segmentation narrows access and protected backups mitigate recovery damage | **Conditionally viable** via approved clinical sessions/services and unfilled C5/C7 coverage |
| **KC4 — Malicious insider → Alaris misuse** | C1 constrains management sources; existing or required vendor-approved minimum safeguards still matter | **Residual insider abuse possible** from a genuinely authorized biomedical position; full C8 deferred; **RISK-004** |
| **KC5 — Compromised vendor → EHR records** | Direct vendor-to-DB and unauthorized AJP/management access should be restricted; C3 monitors available events | **Residual application-layer/vendor trust risk** if a genuinely authorized EHR application or session is compromised; **RISK-001** |

**Selection rationale:** KC2 has the clearest *unclosed human-process failure*, but it is negligence, **not a deliberate BlackReef ransomware technique**. KC3 is the more credible adversarial choice for the requested BlackReef role, with clear caveats about access rights, existing endpoint defenses, segmentation and recovery controls.

### 2. Alternative Attack Path — Five-Step Conditional Sequence

**Objective:** Create imaging-service disruption and possible data-extortion pressure **without relying on unrestricted VPN access, disabled MFA, a flat network or destruction of immutable backups**. This is a high-level, controlled red-team scenario rather than a procedure to execute.

| Step | BlackReef's hypothesized action / decision | Unclosed gap and why T8 controls do not fully address it | Defender's evidence and potential break point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Human entry** | A deceptive message aimed at an authorized Radiology worker results in compromised user access, *if* training and existing defenses fail. | KC3 and **GAP-014**: clinical-role phishing readiness was incomplete; C2 VPN/admin MFA does not establish MFA for every PACS session. | Phishing-report ticket, mail security evidence, identity sign-in review; role-specific drills and stronger application-session authentication |
| **2 — Authorized-session abuse** | The adversary attempts to misuse the compromised user's existing PACS access rather than directly reach restricted server-management ports. | Shared Radiology identity **C-052/GAP-011** and application-level privilege review are outside the five purchased controls; C1 must permit legitimate viewer traffic. | Named-user session/audit correlation, PACS role/permission checks, automatic session revocation, prohibit shared identities |
| **3 — Attempted clinical-service impact** | From the permitted application workflow, the adversary attempts unauthorized access to imaging studies or a service-affecting operation, *only if* the stolen identity has excessive privileges or a separate flaw exists. | **C5 deferred**: no assumed complete EDR on critical PACS servers and unsupported devices; segmentation is not an application authorization control. No particular exploit or permission is asserted as confirmed. | PACS permission-denial tests, supported-host EDR telemetry, clinical change logs, anomaly alerts; remove unnecessary write/delete rights |
| **4 — Delayed containment opportunity** | Suspicious activity could persist longer if it occurs when no analyst is responding, even though Wazuh has collected relevant logs. | **C7 24/7 SOC not purchased**; C3 is an analyst-operated SIEM, and device/vendor log completeness cannot be presumed. | Test after-hours alert-to-human escalation SLA, PACS log coverage and response drill; pre-authorized clinical-safe containment |
| **5 — Extortion/availability consequence** | A temporary PACS outage or exposure of image data could produce patient-care disruption and pressure even if restoration is ultimately possible. | C4's immutable copies **limit destructive leverage** and, under this exercise, approved recovery is functional; they cannot reverse a prior disclosure or guarantee zero downtime. PACS recovery speed and clinical revalidation still govern actual impact. | Restore drill showing images **plus metadata** and permitted DICOM workflow within Radiology-approved RTO/RPO; incident communications and audit trail |

**Attack-path verdict:** This path **may fail at any step**—existing Sophos protection, limited PACS privileges, C3 alerts, application controls and C1 isolation may stop it. It does **not** posit a direct Guest/VPN-to-PACS bypass, disabling the deployed firewall or deleting immutable backup objects. The unpriced or deferred portions of the strategy are the risk exposure, not proof of successful compromise. **Relevant findings:** 027 (15 non-reporting Sophos agents in the original assessment), 004 (adjacent legacy MRI), 024 (unencrypted DICOM transport); none alone proves the PACS application permits abuse.

### 3. Insider Threat That Remains Dangerous (1x01 Task 3)

**Most critical clinical-integrity scenario: a negligent, authorized pharmacy administrator changes dosage values without independent pharmacist approval.** This matches **T3's negligent-insider/unsafe-automation theme**, 1x01 **KC2**, 1x00's historical dosage-change incident, **GAP-002/GAP-022**, and **RISK-005**. The employee already has a legitimate workflow and need not defeat VPN MFA, interzone ACLs or the Westside firewall; a functioning Wazuh sensor may record the change without determining that a medical value is clinically wrong. **C4** restores data *after* a problem but does not guarantee correct dosage at the point of care. The specific defenses are signed two-person pharmacist review, dose/reference-range testing, staged three-site rollout and an application-consistent rollback with clinical verification. These must remain mandatory even if a financial ALE is **not quantified (NQ)**.

**Deliberate insider variant from T3:** An employee with valid EHR access can snoop on or share patient records within ordinary application permissions (**T3 S4**, **GAP-017/GAP-020**, **RISK-007**). Stronger VLANs cannot distinguish curiosity from a legitimate chart lookup; C3 requires sufficiently prompt, attributable audit logs and escalation. The separate KC4 malicious biomedical-insider scenario is likewise relevant because C8's *full* dedicated medical-device monitoring remains deferred; however, minimum vendor-approved credential/source safeguards must not be postponed for patient-safety reasons. **Do not equate every insider event with a malicious actor.**

## Part 2 — The Honest Assessment

### 1. Overall Residual Risk: **CRITICAL**

**Rating method:** Use the **highest material untreated risk**, not an average of ten ratings or a sum of correlated ALE scenarios. The post-T8 **RISK-007 negligent patient-data disclosure** remains **L5 × I4 = 20/25 (Critical)** in [Task 10](10-risk_register.md), because the chosen five controls have no independently budgeted, demonstrated export authorization/DLP and clinical training program that can justify lowering it. **RISK-005** medication-dose integrity and **RISK-004** Alaris patient-safety exposure can also have catastrophic consequences not captured by narrow monetary figures. KC3 remains a conditional ransomware route through authorized application interactions. The five selected controls materially reduce the most direct KC1 network path and improve detection/recovery, **but do not close human authorization, application integrity, all endpoint visibility, or after-hours incident response**.

**Financial cross-check (Task 8 models, not verified outcomes):** Under the exact assumed allocation, the R2 VPN/compound scenario has **$2,214,400/year modeled residual ALE** versus $2,864,400 baseline; the R1 EHR scenario has **$2,935,000/year** versus $3,025,000; R4 billing has about **$90,143/year** versus $135,143. The R3 negligent-insider proxy remains **$300,000/year** without a separately credited control; the R5 pump proxy remains **$70,000/year** before separately evidenced minimum-safety treatments. These scenario losses overlap (especially VPN, EHR and billing), so **never add them to claim a total enterprise residual ALE** or interpret the T8 $785,000 marginal *selection score* as guaranteed annual savings. A Critical overall classification is **a conservative governance judgement**, not an empirical breach forecast.

### 2. Single Biggest Remaining Gap

**The biggest attack-relevant technical gap is incomplete, clinically compatible endpoint/server detection and response coverage on critical application hosts and authorized-user workflows.** T8 funded **C3 Wazuh**, but log collection is not the same as supported host-level prevention/behavioral detection plus a timely human response; **C5** all-estate EDR and **C7** outsourced 24/7 monitoring were not selected. The original assessment recorded **15 non-reporting Sophos agents (Finding 027)** and no assured server/legacy-device agent coverage. The most dangerous failure mode is an authorized clinical session interacting with an important application, while host telemetry, application-level authorization or prompt escalation fails. This is particularly relevant to **RISK-003/KC3**, with spillover to RISK-001/002/008. It does **not** erase separate urgent clinical-policy gaps: the pharmacy two-person change gate, individual EHR export approvals and minimum pump safeguards must proceed regardless of new purchases.

### 3. #1 Priority for Next Year's Budget

**Fund a scoped, evidence-driven C5 critical-host EDR and rapid-response coverage program before a blanket estate-wide purchase.** Start with **PACS and other supported high-risk server/administrative endpoints** and the 15 originally non-reporting Sophos workstations; first validate the existing agent licenses, inventory and compatible operating systems. Seek a vendor quote and pilot for the critical cohort, integrate verified alerts into C3 Wazuh, and contract a modest after-hours **on-call or targeted MDR escalation** if the exercised alert-to-human time fails the agreed SLA. Do **not** assume Sophos can safely run on Windows XP MRI controllers, BD Alaris pumps or any uncertified device; use passive monitoring and vendor-approved isolation instead. **Gate authorization** on measured coverage, safe deployment, detections in benign test exercises and attributable escalation, not on license purchase alone.

**Financial trade-off:** The original **full C5 model** costs **$41,000/year** for only **$43,000/year of *hypothetical marginal* benefit**, a narrow **+$2,000/year** net before quote/efficacy uncertainty; it was reasonable to defer *that scope* in T8. The proposed next-year **scoped phase has no validated price or ARR yet**; reprice, pilot and return a justified business case to Robert Kim rather than inventing savings. A full **C7 SOC at $185,000/year** remained unjustified by its modeled $80,000/year marginal value, so do not simply reverse that earlier rejection. **C8** full medical-device isolation/monitoring ($44,000/year; $35,000/year modeled benefit, −$9,000 financial net) remains a separate urgent patient-safety planning decision that Biomedical and Clinical Leadership must escalate if needed **before** the next budget year. Any mandatory clinical mitigation is not contingent on a positive financial ROI.

**Accountability and next review:** James Chen sponsors the critical coverage plan and tracks KRIs; Sarah Park owns technical inventory, C5 pilot and Wazuh integration; Radiology and Biomedical approve clinical compatibility; DPO/Chief Pharmacist jointly own the immediate unauthorized-export and dose-change safeguards; Robert Kim approves new costs. Demonstrate PACS role separation, supported endpoint coverage, tested after-hours escalation and clinical-safe restores; update RISK-003/004/005/007 and the entire Risk Register at its next scheduled review or immediately after a KRI threshold breach. No residual score is lowered without documented test evidence and risk-owner approval.

## Conclusion for the Board

The **$97,000/year T8 blueprint materially improves MedDefense**: it breaks broad VPN-to-server trust, segments AD/EHR/medical systems, gives analysts centralized alerts and creates defensible recovery options. **BlackReef does not need the old flat network if a legitimate clinical account and an allowed application workflow are still abused**, and insiders can create patient-harming errors without acting maliciously. The correct conclusion is therefore **Critical residual risk under a highest-untreated-risk method**, not that the program failed: maintain the funded controls, close immediate patient-safety and privacy process gaps, and build a measured next-year critical-host detection/response investment rather than promising zero risk.
