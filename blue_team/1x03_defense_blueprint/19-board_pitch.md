# MedDefense Health Systems — Board Pitch

## 1. Current State

After four weeks of assessment, MedDefense's overall cybersecurity risk remains **Critical**: remote access and internal network permissions are too broad, protection coverage is uneven, and recovery of critical clinical services is not proven. Our register tracks ten major risks, including a Critical patient-information disclosure risk. **Verdict: We know where we are exposed, but proposed safeguards have not yet been implemented.**

## 2. The Risk

Consider stolen VPN credentials enabling ransomware to spread through the central identity system, disrupting patient records and billing. The modeled loss for one full incident is **$9,548,000**; at an assumed frequency of **0.30 incidents/year**, the expected annual loss is **$2,864,400** ($9,548,000 × 0.30). This is a planning scenario, not a measured forecast.

## 3. The Plan

Prioritize **multifactor authentication** (a second sign-in check), **network segmentation** (blocking unnecessary movement between systems), and **Wazuh monitoring** (centralized security alerts): **$63,000/year** in annualized costs for **$705,000/year** in modeled, scenario-allocated risk-reduction scores. Add tamper-resistant offsite backups and a managed Westside firewall, bringing the five-control package to **$97,000/year** in annualized cost. Upfront equipment makes estimated **first-year cash $115,000**, nominally $5,000 below the **$120,000 six-month program ceiling** before unpriced staffing, vendor, privacy, and clinical costs. Approve the direction and **staged spending up to $120,000 only after** Finance confirms the complete six-month cash schedule and clinical teams approve safe changes.

## 4. The Return

Under the planning model, **$785,000/year** in marginal risk-reduction scores divided by **$97,000/year** in annualized costs equals **about $8.09 of potentially avoided annual loss per $1 invested**, or **$688,000/year modeled net value**. These overlapping scenario estimates are **not guaranteed savings**; success requires tested access restrictions, actionable alerts, verified recovery, and separate patient-safety and privacy protections.
